EVOLUTION & THE ORIGIN OF COMPLEXITY · EXPERIMENT 01

Emergence Lab

A miniature world where agents inherit simple decision weights, gather energy, encounter hazards, and reproduce with mutation. Does natural selection produce adaptive behavior without anybody programming a desire to survive?

Not a consciousness detector

The world

AgentsFoodHazardEmpty

All agents follow the same movement algorithm. The four numbers controlling attraction to food, response to danger, persistence, and last successful direction start random. Offspring inherit their parent's numbers with possible mutation. The control assigns newborns fresh random numbers instead. “Blind” agents cannot read nearby food or hazard cues. The memoryless control removes the influence of recent direction and reward history.

What's programmed

Physical rules, local sensing, movement, energy costs, inheritance, and reproduction. The agents have an explicitly designed internal state (recent movement/reward). We do not pretend memory appears from nothing.

What's selected

The genome weights are randomized rather than set to “seek food” or “avoid danger.” If variants with particular weights leave more descendants, the population's weights can drift toward those variants.

What isn't measured

Subjective experience. The model tests a pathway toward adaptive sensing, not whether a simulated organism feels anything. Complex behaviors do not, by themselves, demonstrate consciousness.
